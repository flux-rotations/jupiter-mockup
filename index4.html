— Key Changes —
1
Unify UNOS into one position
Token row shows total 240,142 UNOS with a one-line breakdown: 104,681 available · 135,461 locked. No more "where did my tokens go" — every UNOS is accounted for in the same place George is already looking.
2
Flag tokens with active orders
A small 1 order chip on the token name makes it scannable at a glance which holdings have funds tied up off-wallet — without forcing the user into the second pill view to find out.
3
Rename "Jupiter" → "Open Orders"
A section titled "Jupiter" inside the Jupiter app is recursive and meaningless to anyone who doesn't already know it refers to the protocol's order vault. Functional names beat brand names.
4
One card, five order-aware templates
Same component, content adapts per type. Limit → target vs. current + fill. DCA → schedule + next buy + acquired + avg cost. VA → target value vs. position value + deviation. Trigger → distance-to-trigger. Perps → entry/mark/liq + unrealized P&L + liquidation distance. Color-coded badges per type so the eye sorts them instantly.
5
Staking joins the same model
Staked JUP appears two places — as a breakdown row inside the JUP holding (with a pink-dot indicator that distinguishes "staked" from "locked in order"), and as its own card in a Staking section with APR, claimable rewards, cooldown, and voting power. Same component family, no new mental model.
6
Reconcile the top-line total optically
The disconnect between the top balance and the visible token list is what confused George. Every dollar now traces to a visible source — wallet balance, locked-in-order, accumulating-from-DCA, staked, or perps collateral. Nothing hidden behind unlabeled icons.
7
Scales without new patterns
The same three primitives — holding row, breakdown sub-row, product card — handle Limit, DCA, VA, Trigger, Perps, and Staking. Future products (JupSOL liquid staking, Perps TP/SL, future lending) drop into the same scaffold. Lending and borrowing were considered but excluded here to keep the demo focused.
8
USDC becomes a first-class holding
DCA, VA, and Perps all draw collateral or budget from USDC. In the current app, that USDC simply disappears once committed. In the proposed view, USDC is its own row showing exactly where each dollar is reserved — $145 wallet · $360 DCA · $35 VA · $25 perps. The "$25 collateral with no USDC holding" math problem goes away.
9
Holdings are collapsible
Each token row with a breakdown can be collapsed via the chevron — the screen stays clean for users who don't need the detail, and expandable for users who do. Default is expanded so the design value is immediately visible.
10
Tap-to-jump linking
Every chip and locked-row in Holdings is a deep link to the related product card. Tap "1 order" on UNOS → scrolls to and pulses the limit order. Tap "DCA budget" inside USDC → scrolls to the DCA card. Holdings answer what; product cards answer why, and one tap bridges them.
